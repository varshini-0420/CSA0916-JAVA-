TASK 1 – UNDERSTAND THE PROBLEM AND IDENTIFY THE OBJECTS

1. Problem Analysis

The Campus Smart-Parking System manages a multi-level parking facility used by students, staff and visitors. The system allocates a suitable free parking slot when a vehicle enters, creates a parking ticket, records the entry time and calculates the parking fee when the vehicle leaves.

2. Candidate Classes and Their Responsibilities

Vehicle
Represents a vehicle entering the parking facility. It stores the registration number and vehicle type. Different vehicle types can have different parking rates.

ParkingSlot
Represents an individual parking slot. It stores the slot number and whether the slot is free or occupied.

ParkingFacility
Represents the complete parking area. It manages multiple floors and searches for available parking slots.

ParkingTicket
Represents the ticket issued to a vehicle. It contains a unique ticket number, vehicle details, allocated slot and entry time.

User
Represents a person using the parking facility. The user can be a Student, Staff member or Visitor.

Student and Staff
These users may have a valid monthly pass and can park free of charge.

Visitor
Represents a visitor who pays the normal parking fee.

3. Business Rules

1. A parking slot cannot contain more than one vehicle at the same time.
2. A vehicle can occupy only one parking slot.
3. A ticket number must be unique and must not be reused.
4. A vehicle must be allocated a suitable free slot before entering.
5. When a vehicle leaves, its parking slot must be released.
6. Parking fees depend on the vehicle type and parking duration.
7. Students and staff with a valid monthly pass can park free of charge.
8. Visitors must pay the applicable parking fee.
9. An invalid slot number cannot be allocated or released.

4. Assumptions

1. Each vehicle has a unique registration number.
2. Each floor has a fixed number of parking slots.
3. A vehicle cannot enter the parking facility twice at the same time.
4. Entry and exit times are recorded by the system.
5. The parking facility has a limited number of slots.

5. Alternative Concept – Parallel Arrays

Instead of representing each vehicle as an object, the system could use parallel arrays:

regNo[]
type[]
entryTime[]

For example, information about one vehicle would be stored at the same index in all arrays.

Finding a Vehicle:
Both a simple object array and parallel arrays require a linear search when searching by registration number. Therefore, the time complexity is O(n).

Adding a New Vehicle Attribute:
In an object-oriented design, a new attribute can be added to the Vehicle class. In parallel arrays, a new array must be created and maintained at the correct index.

Space Complexity:
Both approaches require O(n) space for n vehicles. However, the object-oriented design is easier to maintain because all information related to one vehicle is grouped together in one object.
